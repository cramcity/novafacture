How to follow up on an unpaid invoice

Most late payments are simply forgotten. A quick, polite and specific reminder usually works. Here is a follow-up timeline and email templates you can copy.

  1. Check your invoice — Make sure it is complete (number, date, due date, payment details) and that it reached the right person.
  2. Day 1 after due: friendly reminder — A short, polite email with the invoice number, amount, due date and the invoice attached.
  3. Day 7: firm reminder — Ask for a specific payment date. A phone call at this stage often settles it.
  4. Day 15–30: final notice — A formal letter or email giving a deadline (e.g. 7 days) and stating the next steps. Keep a copy.
  5. Apply late fees if allowed — Charge only what your terms or the law allow. In the UK, for business-to-business debts, you can claim statutory interest of 8% above the Bank of England base rate plus fixed compensation (£40 to £100 depending on the amount).
  6. Last resort: small claims — Most countries have a simple small-claims procedure (for example the small claims track in England and Wales, or provincial small claims courts in Canada). Check the limits and fees where you live.

Copy-paste reminder emails

Friendly reminder (day 1)
Subject: Invoice [number] — friendly reminder

Hi [name],

Just a quick reminder that invoice [number] for [amount], due on [date], is still open. I've attached it again for convenience.

If you've already sent payment, please ignore this message.

Thanks,
[your name]
Firm reminder (day 7)
Subject: Invoice [number] — payment overdue

Hi [name],

Invoice [number] for [amount] was due on [date] and remains unpaid.

Could you let me know when payment will be made? Happy to help if anything on the invoice needs clarifying.

Best regards,
[your name]
Final notice
Subject: Invoice [number] — final notice

Hi [name],

Despite my reminders of [date] and [date], invoice [number] for [amount] is still unpaid.

Please settle it by [deadline]. After that date I will apply the late fees in my terms and consider further steps to recover the debt.

Regards,
[your name]
