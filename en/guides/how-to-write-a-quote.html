How to write a quote or estimate, step by step

A quote fixes the price and scope of your work before you start; an estimate is an approximate price that may change. Either way, a clear document avoids misunderstandings and helps the client say yes.

  1. Your details and the client's — As on an invoice: name, address, contact details and any tax registration number.
  2. A number and a date — Number your quotes (e.g. Q-2026-014) so you can find them and turn them into invoices.
  3. Itemise the work — Each deliverable with quantity and price. State what is included and what is not (e.g. number of revisions).
  4. Tax and total — Subtotal, each applicable tax with its rate, and the total.
  5. Validity period — For example 30 days: after that, your prices or availability may change.
  6. Terms and acceptance — Deposit (often 30–50%), timeline, payment terms, and a line for the client to sign and date to accept.
Tip

Follow up on an unanswered quote after 7 days with a simple question such as 'Do the budget and timeline work for you?'.
