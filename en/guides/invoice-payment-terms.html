Invoice payment terms explained (Net 30 and more)

Payment terms tell your client when and how to pay. Clear terms, agreed before the work starts and repeated on the invoice, are one of the simplest ways to get paid on time.

  1. Due on receipt — Payment is expected as soon as the client receives the invoice. Common for small jobs and new clients.
  2. Net 7, Net 15, Net 30, Net 60 — The full amount is due 7, 15, 30 or 60 days after the invoice date. Net 30 is the most common between businesses; shorter terms are usually paid faster.
  3. EOM (end of month) — Payment is due at the end of the month in which the invoice is issued. 'Net 30 EOM' means 30 days after the end of that month.
  4. 2/10 Net 30 — An early-payment discount: the client may deduct 2% if they pay within 10 days; otherwise the full amount is due within 30 days.
  5. Late fees — State any late fee or interest on the invoice and only charge what your agreement and local law allow. In the UK, for business-to-business debts, statutory interest of 8% above the Bank of England base rate plus a fixed £40–£100 compensation applies if you have not agreed otherwise.
  6. Write an exact due date — Next to the term, add the actual date (e.g. 'Net 15 — due by December 11, 2026') so there is no room for interpretation.

Copy-paste wording

Standard
Payment terms: Net 15. Please pay by [date].
Accepted methods: bank transfer, PayPal.
Please include invoice number [number] in the payment reference.
With late fee
Payment terms: Net 30. Please pay by [date].
Overdue balances incur interest of [x]% per month from the due date, as agreed in our contract.
With early-payment discount
Payment terms: 2/10 Net 30. Deduct 2% if paid by [date + 10 days]; otherwise the full amount is due by [date + 30 days].
