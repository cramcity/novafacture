How to number your invoices

Every invoice needs its own number. In the UK, a VAT invoice must carry a unique number that follows on from the last one; in the US, Canada and Australia no specific format is imposed, but unique sequential numbers are standard practice and make payments and audits far easier to track.

  1. Make every number unique — Never reuse an invoice number, even for a different client.
  2. Keep the sequence in order — Number invoices in the order you issue them, with no gaps: after 2026-014 comes 2026-015.
  3. Add a year prefix if you like — A format such as 2026-001 lets you restart at 001 each year without creating duplicates, because the year is part of the number. 202611-001 (year + month) also works.
  4. Use separate series only when needed — You can run separate sequences (e.g. INV-2026-001 for invoices and CN-2026-001 for credit notes), as long as each series is itself continuous.
  5. Never delete an issued number — If an invoice was sent by mistake, cancel it with a credit note that refers to its number instead of deleting it, so your sequence stays complete.
  6. Keep quotes out of the sequence — Quotes and pro forma invoices are not invoices: give them their own reference (e.g. Q-2026-014 or PF-2026-003).
