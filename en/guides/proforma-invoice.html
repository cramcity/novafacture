Pro forma invoice: what it is and how to make one

A pro forma invoice is a preliminary bill sent before the work is done or the goods are shipped. It shows the buyer exactly what the final invoice will look like, but it is not a tax invoice and does not record a sale.

  1. Know when to use it — To request an advance payment or deposit, to let a client get internal approval or a purchase order, or to declare the value of goods to customs before shipping.
  2. Title it clearly — Write 'Pro forma invoice' at the top so nobody mistakes it for a final invoice.
  3. Use a separate number — Give it its own reference (e.g. PF-2026-003) and keep it out of your invoice number sequence.
  4. Include the full details — Your details and the client's, item descriptions, quantities, prices, applicable tax, total, delivery terms and, for exports, origin, weight and HS codes if known.
  5. Add validity and payment terms — How long the prices are valid, the deposit or full payment required, and accepted payment methods.
  6. Issue the real invoice afterwards — When the goods ship or the work is done, issue a regular invoice with its own sequential number. That invoice, not the pro forma, is what you and your client use for tax records (VAT/GST claims).
Tip

Pro forma vs quote: a quote proposes a price for the client to accept; a pro forma is usually sent once the deal is agreed, to collect payment or clear customs.
