How to charge late fees on an invoice

Late fees encourage clients to pay on time, but they only work if they are agreed in advance and written clearly. Here is how to set them up properly, with the main rules in the UK, the US and Canada.

  1. Agree the fee before you start — Put your late fee in your contract, quote or terms and conditions, and get the client to accept them before the work starts. A fee added only after the invoice is overdue is hard to enforce.
  2. State it on every invoice — Write the due date and the fee next to it, for example: 'Payment due within 30 days. Late payments incur interest of 1.5% per month (18% per year).'
  3. UK: you can claim statutory interest — For business-to-business debts, the Late Payment of Commercial Debts (Interest) Act 1998 lets you claim simple interest at 8% above the 'reference rate', plus fixed compensation of £40 (debt under £1,000), £70 (£1,000 to £9,999.99) or £100 (£10,000 or more). The reference rate is the Bank of England base rate on 30 June (for debts that become late from 1 July to 31 December) or on 31 December (for debts that become late from 1 January to 30 June). For debts that became late in the second half of 2026 it is 3.75%, so statutory interest is 11.75% per year.
  4. US: check your state's limits — There is no single federal rule. States cap interest rates and some limit late fees, so keep your rate reasonable (1% to 1.5% per month is common) and check your state's rules.
  5. Canada: show the annual rate — Under the federal Interest Act, if you state interest only as a monthly rate without its yearly equivalent, you can generally recover no more than 5% per year. Write both, for example '1.5% per month (18% per year)'.
  6. Remind before you charge — Send a polite reminder a few days after the due date first. Many late payments are oversights, and a friendly reminder keeps the relationship intact; apply the fee if the invoice stays unpaid.

Frequently asked questions

Can I charge late fees to individual customers in the UK?

The 1998 Act only covers business-to-business debts. With consumers, you can only charge interest or a late fee if it is in the contract they agreed to, and the term must be fair and transparent under the Consumer Rights Act 2015.

What will the UK statutory rate be in early 2027?

For debts that become late from 1 January to 30 June 2027, the rate is 8% plus the Bank of England base rate on 31 December 2026. The base rate has been 4% since 5 November 2026 and the next decision is due on 17 December 2026, so check the rate after that date.

Is UK late payment law changing?

The Commercial Payments Bill, introduced to Parliament on 19 May 2026, proposes to cap most commercial payment terms at 60 days and to stop contracts from excluding statutory interest. It was not yet law in November 2026: until it is passed and in force, the 1998 Act applies as described above.

Is 1.5% per month really 18% per year?

Yes, as simple interest (1.5% × 12). If you compound monthly, the effective rate is about 19.6% per year. In Canada, the Interest Act requires the yearly rate to be stated; otherwise you can generally recover no more than 5% per year.

How much can I charge in the US?

Each state sets its own limits through usury and consumer protection laws, and the limits are often different for business and consumer clients. A rate of 1% to 1.5% per month is common, but check your state's maximum and put the rate in your contract before you start.
