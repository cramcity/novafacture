Invoice vs receipt: the difference and when to use each

An invoice and a receipt look similar but do opposite jobs. The invoice asks the client to pay; the receipt confirms the payment was received. Many freelancers need both: an invoice when the work is delivered, and a receipt when a client pays on the spot or asks for proof of payment.

Create an invoice or receipt

  1. Send an invoice before payment — It lists what you delivered, the amount due, tax, the due date and how to pay. It is a request for payment.
  2. Issue a receipt after payment — It confirms the amount received, the date, the payment method and what it was for. It is proof that the amount has been paid.
  3. Include the right details on each — Both show your details, the client's, a unique number and the amount. The invoice adds a due date and payment instructions; the receipt adds the payment date and method.
  4. Know when a receipt is enough — For an immediate payment (cash or card on the spot), a receipt alone is common. For work billed in advance or on payment terms, start with an invoice.
  5. Link the two documents — Mention the invoice number on the receipt so both documents match in your records and the client's.
  6. Keep copies of both — Tax authorities expect you to keep sales records for several years (for example 6 years in Canada and generally 5 to 6 years in the UK; in the US, usually at least 3 years).
